How I work

The principles behind how I approach infrastructure, wherever I'm working.

Reliability over noise

Monitoring should catch real issues, not generate alerts nobody trusts. I'd rather have fewer, meaningful alerts than a dashboard full of things everyone's learned to ignore.

Zabbix Alerting Runbooks

Automate what pays off

Not automation for its own sake — the point is removing genuinely repetitive toil, handling edge cases properly, and leaving something the next person can actually maintain.

Ansible Bash CI/CD

Documentation as insurance

If I'm the only person who knows how something works, that's a risk, not job security. Good docs mean the team isn't stuck waiting on one person at 3am.

Knowledge base Handover

Access should be scoped, not assumed

Provisioning access, reviewing what's actually still needed, and closing gaps before they become incidents — not glamorous, but it's most of what actually keeps a business safe.

What this looks like day to day

The same two things, regardless of what I'm actually working on.

Ops-first

Experience running Linux at scale: monitoring (Zabbix + Pingdom), config management (Ansible), and making changes that don't break things at 2am.

Clear communication

Status updates and human explanations, not just logs and graphs. Whoever I'm working with knows what's happening and why.

How I approach a problem

Same process whether it's a five-minute fix or a multi-week project.

1

Understand it

What's actually broken, who it affects, and how urgent it really is — before touching anything.

2

Plan the change

What I'm going to do, what could go wrong, and how to roll it back if it does.

3

Execute carefully

Make the change, verify it actually worked, watch for knock-on effects.

4

Document it

So the next person — possibly future me — isn't starting from zero.
